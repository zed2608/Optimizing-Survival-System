## Wording of the "potential" layer (decision of 12 Oct 2026, Ayzhiedy)
- If a layer built from satellite tree cover is ever used or mentioned, call it only **"environmental support for woody cover (satellite-derived, provisional)"**.
- It is **not** a survival measure and must never be described as one.
- It uses the model's *predicted* value from the environment (elevation, slope, soil), never the observed tree cover, and never penalises open land by its observed cover.
- No gap or restoration score for now.

Status: the pre-registered benchmark (`docs/defense/POTENTIAL_BENCHMARK.md`, 12 Oct 2026) says DROP; no screen, Help text, README or CLAUDE.md sentence uses the term yet. If the layer is built later, the wording above has to be added to Help and the user guide, `README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/defense/ALGORITHMS.md`, `LIMITATIONS.md` and `PANEL_QA.md`.

## Potential layer: verdict of the pre-registered benchmark (12 Oct 2026): DROP
Accepted by Ayzhiedy. Steps 4 to 7 (the switch, labels, outputs) are NOT built. No rule or setting may be changed to alter the verdict; a different setup would be a new, labelled run. The rules, thresholds and the numbers below are pinned by `tests/test_potential_benchmark.py`. Source: `docs/defense/POTENTIAL_BENCHMARK.md`, `scripts/potential_benchmark.py`.

**The three rules and the numbers**
- **R1 (forest at least 0.10 R-squared above EACH of Decision Tree, linear regression and KNN, and better in at least 4 of 5 folds): FAILED.** Forest above the Decision Tree by +0.040 and above KNN by +0.084 (needed 0.10); above linear regression by +0.158 (passes); better than each of them in 5 of 5 folds.
- **R2 (forest R-squared of the main model, environment only, at least 0.30): FAILED at 0.29999** (folds 0.067, 0.354, 0.614, 0.337, 0.128; standard deviation about 0.22). The drop does not depend on this rounding: R1 fails by a wide margin.
- **R3 (|Spearman| with the rule score at most 0.80): passed at -0.221** (-0.315 with the number of eligible species).

**The 430-square finding (descriptive, not a drop rule).** On the 430 squares with tree cover below 0.5 (grassland and cropland) the forest's predicted potential separates them from the rest with an AUROC of 0.865. The lowest 10% of predicted potential holds 38.8% low-cover squares (base rate 5.9%) and catches 66.0% of all of them; the mean predicted potential is 0.66 for them and 0.95 for the others. So even a prediction from elevation, slope and soil tracks where open land already is, which conflicts with "never penalize open land".

**The decision-gate numbers (rf source, Hungarian, nothing built; 534 barangay plans and 893 drawn-polygon plans).**
- Placed blocks and trees changed in 0 of 1,427 plans in every variant (by construction: S and the 0.50 test are untouched).
- Tie-break: 5,519 of 10,167 blocks (54.3%) of barangay plans and 3,698 of 9,247 (40.0%) of polygon plans moved to other squares; total W change 0.0000%; the blocks spread out (mean distance from the area centre 1.67 and 1.50 times larger); 89.4% and 80.7% of chosen blocks have another equally scored square.
- adjust_w factors 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.20 (none chosen): 41% to 49% of blocks moved; total W -0.009% / -0.024% / -0.034% (barangay plans) and -0.002% / -0.003% / -0.005% (polygon plans); worst single plan -1.5%.

**The sentence we can keep:** "a nonlinear model predicts real tree cover clearly better than a linear one, but the forest is not clearly better than a single tree or KNN."

**Do not quote the R-squared of 0.435.** The estimate of 0.435 (0.405 without latitude and longitude; Decision Tree 0.066 and 0.252; linear 0.282) in section 4b of `docs/HANDOFF_2026-10-12.md` came from a rougher setup in the chat session (an untuned fixed-setting run that used `nearest_feature_dist_m` and 7,392 squares). The registered benchmark gives 0.300 for the forest on 7,322 squares. The handoff is historical and was left unchanged.

## Estimating soil for the squares without soil: NOT pursued (decision of 12 Oct 2026, Ayzhiedy)
Considered: use the Random Forest to estimate the soil texture of the squares that have none in the LGU soil map, and compare it with simple baselines. **Decision: the benchmark is not run and no script was written.** The reasons are the read-only findings below (nothing was changed or computed into any file).

**Where the squares without soil are**
- 3,393 of 8,088 grid squares have no soil texture; all of them are scored squares (3,393 of 8,010). 3,392 lie where the digitized LGU map has no coloured pixel (the map leaves the upper watershed white: outside the map, not digitization gaps); 1 is a partial gap. Zoning: 2,173 confirmed and 1,220 outside our zoning map.
- They form ONE block: 3,384 squares (99.7%), plus 9 squares in 4 small blocks (2 single squares, 2 blocks of 2 to 4). By barangay: Pintong Bukawe 3,389 of its 4,591 squares (73.8%), Banaba 3, Silangan 1, the other 12 barangays none.
- Distance of each such square to the nearest square with soil: median 2.1 km (2,059 m), 90th percentile 5.2 km, 99th percentile 7.2 km, maximum 8.1 km; 233 squares within 300 m, 600 within 300 m to 1 km, 1,450 within 1 to 3 km, 1,110 farther than 3 km.
- The land is steeper than the mapped area: median slope 28% against 14% (elevation overlaps: median 215 m against 200 m; 5% of the squares outside the 1st to 99th percentile elevation range of the mapped squares, 3% for slope).
- It is forest land: 2,105 of the squares are in the confirmed Forest Zone (62%), 1,220 lie outside our zoning map and carry the Forest Reserve flag (36%), and only 68 are in other zones (64 Institutional Research, 4 High Density Residential - Mixed Use).

**Classes of the known squares**
- Of the 4,695 squares with soil texture: Clay 3,692 (78.6%), Silt Loam 502, Loam 254, Clay Loam 172, Sandy Loam 75 (imbalance 49 to 1). Only 4,397 come from the map itself; 298 were filled from a neighbour within 300 m (`soil_filled`) and the system treats them as known. Seven series (Antipolo Clay 2,903 down to Novaliches Clay Loam 26).
- Soil follows elevation in the known area: Clay has a median elevation of 267 m, the other four classes 18 to 36 m.

**How the rules treat missing soil today**
- For 44 of the 45 species (the one species that accepts any texture is unaffected) the soil term is NOT evaluated on these squares: the other three terms are re-weighted, the confidence drops, and there is no mismatch penalty. The Random Forest copies this (an "unknown soil" input), so `s_prob` does the same.

**The most an estimate could change (an upper bound, rule S in memory, 152,685 pairs in these squares, 90,120 eligible now; nothing saved)**
- S would change in 52,702 to 86,507 pairs (mean change 0.04 to 0.085, maximum 0.188), depending on the class assumed.
- Eligibility (S >= 0.50) could flip in up to 4,230 pairs (2.8%) in up to 651 of the 3,393 squares (19%); if every square were Clay: 2,253 pairs lost, 238 gained. The Random Forest would need retraining (the rebuild takes about 278 s).

**Why it is not pursued**
- This is not gap-filling but predicting one 34 km² block up to 8 km from the nearest known soil, on steeper land than the model could learn from; the majority class is already 78.6%, so a forest would most likely only reproduce "upland = Clay" (an expectation; it was not tested).
- The white area of the map may be unmapped or mountain soil that is not one of the five classes (UNKNOWN), and the land is forest land (Forest Zone and flagged Forest Reserve), for which the zoning and the MENRO and DENR permits matter more than the texture.
- The LGU soil map is itself provisional (digitized by us, not signed by the agriculturist), so an estimate would stack a guess on a provisional layer and would not improve the survival evidence.
- What stays as it is: squares without soil are scored without the soil term and labelled "Data Unavailable (outside the LGU soil map)"; nothing is estimated or invented.

## Field pack, Stage A1 (branch field-pack, 13 Oct 2026): one kit -> one phone page (prototype)
Built and tested; not deployed, not tested on a real phone, nothing in README / CLAUDE.md / Help mentions it yet. Offline use (Stage A2), hosting (A3) and the real-phone and GPS tests (A4) are NOT done.
- **What it is:** `python scripts/build_field_pack.py <kit .zip or folder> --out <folder>` packs the `blocks.csv` + `manifest.json` of a dashboard field kit into ONE self-contained HTML page (`fieldpack_<plan_id>_<check_code>.html`, about 46 KB for the sample kit of 6 blocks and 50 trees; no network calls, no login). Source: `fieldpack/` (`core.js` maths and rules, `strings.js` every visible text, `app.js` page, `app.css`, `template.html`). The page shows a permanent banner "Prototype. GPS accuracy not yet field-tested.".
- **Tree positions are not computed in the browser.** The build script gives every tree its lat/lon from `palettes.block_geometry` (the one layout formula) and the transform of `field_kit.attach_geometry`, and refuses a kit whose own START / first-tree columns disagree (more than 2e-6 degrees, about 20 cm). The kit's `utm_e` / `utm_n` are rounded to 0.1 m, so the page's trees can differ from the kit's own `first_tree_lat/lon` by a few centimetres (tested: under 25 cm, in practice about 5 cm).
- **Page rules:** next unplanted tree is the target (order of the kit README: row by row, east first); distance and bearing (haversine, same sphere as `field_verify.distance_m`); "arrived" only when distance is within the larger of 2 m and the phone's reported accuracy; distance digits follow the accuracy ("about 25 m", "less than 10 m"); accuracy worse than the tree spacing says the position is only approximate; no compass heading = degrees and N/E/S/W only (arrow only with a live compass or movement heading); planted / skipped with the 8 README reasons; marks kept in `localStorage` inside try/catch (a phone that refuses storage keeps them in memory and warns).
- **Results file:** the 32 columns of `blocks.csv` in the same order, one row per block, then `reason, note, observer, observed_at, gps_lat, gps_lon, gps_accuracy_m, tree_log`. A block with at least one planted tree = `planted` with the count; all trees skipped = the reason word; anything else stays empty (not decided). `moved_lat` / `moved_lon` are always EMPTY: they mean the real centre of the BLOCK, which one tree's position does not tell. The existing import (`POST /field-checks/import`) accepts the file (tested end to end on a real plan: 4 blocks accepted, repeat adds nothing, a wrong check code is refused). It ignores `gps_*` and `tree_log` today; keeping them needs the small additive change of `import_kit_csv` that the project owner approved but that is NOT yet made.
- **GPS from a file:** desktop Edge (headless, fake GPS) gave readings both from `file://` and from `http://127.0.0.1`. That says nothing about phones: the page tries GPS either way and, only if it fails, adds that a page opened from a file may not get GPS and that the https link should be used. To be tested on real phones in A4.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_field_pack.py` (14), `fieldpack/core.test.mjs` (10, run by pytest), `tests/browser/fieldpack_check.mjs` (17 checks in headless Edge at 360 px; run by hand, not by pytest), fixture `tests/fixtures/field_pack/field_kit_plan_urban_20261013_032648/` (blocks.csv + manifest.json of the sample kit; its block coordinates are now in the public repository).

## Field pack: block layout finding and the "null" fix (branch field-pack, 13 Oct 2026)
- **Finding (read-only, nothing changed):** `palettes.block_geometry` places trees 1..N row by row from the south-west corner of the planted rectangle, east first, then one spacing north. When N is smaller than the trees per row, ALL trees stand in the first (south) row: sample kit CHE-B01, 8 trees on an 11 x 11 grid, all at y = 15.0 m, x 15 to 64 m, in a rectangle that runs from 11.5 to 88.5 m. The kit README, `blocks.csv` (first tree, START corner), the GPX / KML corner waypoints, the PDF diagrams, the dashboard `BlockLayout` (through the JavaScript mirror `blockGeometry.js`) and the Field Pack all use that one function, so they agree. It is the documented rule, not a Field Pack bug.
- **Options considered** (all are subsets of the species grid, so no two trees are ever closer than the block spacing; for all 45 species the block spacing is not below the species' own minimum):
  - **A. Centred compact sub-block** (about ceil(sqrt N) columns, row by row, in the middle of the square): neighbours stay at exactly the species spacing; the START corner and the planted rectangle change (so `start_lat/lon`, `rect_side_m`, `margin_m`, the four corner waypoints, the KML polygon, the PDF and the README steps 1 to 4 change; two trailing columns for the planted rows and per-row would be appended); easiest for the planter.
  - **B. N cells picked evenly by index:** irregular, neighbours 1.4 to 3.6 times the spacing in the samples, some pairs farther apart than the species maximum; the planter needs a (column, row) list because there is no row pattern.
  - **C. Regular lattice (every s-th cell and row):** easy to walk with a tape but neighbours are s times the spacing (CHE 35 m, IND 33 m, KAM 25 m): at N = 8 it breaks the species' own MAXIMUM spacing in **43 of 45** species' blocks, at N = 20 in 19 of 43, at N = 50 in 11 of 23.
  - Every option changes `block_geometry`, `blockGeometry.js`, `tests/test_kit_geometry.py`, `blockGeometry.test.mjs`, the order test in `tests/test_field_pack.py` and the sample fixture (the pack builder refuses a kit whose first-tree column disagrees with the formula by more than about 20 cm).
- **Recommendation: A**, in principle the project owner's choice too, **pending the adviser and the agriculturist. NOT implemented.** Frozen documents to update if it is adopted: the kit README text, `docs/PLANTING_BLOCKS.md`, the round 13 section of `CLAUDE.md`.
- **No change to the release hash or to the v1.0-review outputs under any option:** geometry is worked out after planning from existing plan columns, so the species files (hash `34964a09fe44`), `species_clean`, S / P / W, the scores, the matching, the block counts and the saved plan CSV (and so every check code) stay as they are. What would go stale are generated artefacts: kits, PDFs and Field Packs already built, and values derived at read time (`first_tree_m`, `rect_side_m`, `margin_m`).
- **The "null" bug:** the Field Pack block screen showed the word "null" under Start GPS when the page was opened over http (not as a file). Cause: `kids.push(button, cond ? note : null)` followed by `targetBox.replaceChildren(...kids)`; a null passed to `replaceChildren` becomes the text "null". Fixed in `fieldpack/app.js`: every list goes through `clean()` (no null, undefined or true/false), `h()` skips them, the one conditional push is a plain `if`, and the reason text of a tree button is only looked up for a skipped tree. New browser checks look for the words null / undefined / NaN and for missing text keys ("[key]") and unfilled values ("{name}") on the home screen, the block screen before GPS, just after tapping Start GPS, with a GPS reading, and after marking trees; they FAILED on the old code (exactly the one place) and pass now over http and as a file (whole flow 22 checks, map 27 checks). Plus a source test in `tests/test_field_pack.py`. The A1 test had missed it because it opened the page as a file, where the note replaces the null.

- **Heading (in this order):** (a) the phone's compass: Android `deviceorientationabsolute` (and `deviceorientation` when it is flagged absolute) turned into a compass heading with the W3C rotation matrix (phone held flat: the way its top points plus the screen angle; held upright: the way its back faces), iOS `webkitCompassHeading` after `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission()` called inside the tap on Start GPS (the listeners are attached at once and never wait for the permission); (b) the GPS heading when the speed is at least 1 m/s; (c) none: the cone is hidden and the page says "Heading not available". A source that goes quiet is dropped after 2.5 s (compass) or 6 s (GPS heading). Smoothing is a low-pass filter on the circle (alpha 0.25 compass, 0.5 GPS; 359 followed by 1 moves 2 degrees, never 358). The cone is a pie slice on the blue dot: half-width 28 degrees for a steady compass, 50 when the compass is unsteady (circular standard deviation of the last 12 readings above 15 degrees), 12 for the GPS heading while walking (18 below 1.5 m/s). Hint: "If the arrow is wrong, move your phone in a figure 8 to calibrate."
- **Turn cue** (only when a heading exists): within 15 degrees "Straight ahead", 150 degrees or more "Turn around", else "Turn left / right N degrees".
- **Heading-up map: how rotation was done.** Leaflet cannot rotate. Chosen: a CSS rotation of the map container, no plugin (no new licence or third-party code). In heading-up mode the container is a square big enough to cover the stage from every angle, centred on the point that stays put on the screen (the middle of the part the sheet does not cover) and turned by minus the heading; markers, labels and the distance label are turned back with `rotate(calc(-1 * var(--maprot)))` so they stay upright, and the cone, which lives inside the map, needs no correction. Because the rotation point is the user's place, no re-centring is needed when the heading changes (only on each GPS reading). Dragging the map is switched off in heading-up mode (Leaflet's pointer maths does not know about the rotation) and pinch zoom is made to zoom about the centre; Zoom in / out buttons work; heading-up switches Follow on, and turning Follow off or losing the heading goes back to north-up. North-up (the default) uses a normal container and leaves dragging alone. The credit line is NOT inside the rotated container: it is a separate line at the top edge of the sheet. Known weak points: tiles are also loaded for the bigger square (about 3 times the area) only while heading-up is on; not tested on a real phone.
- **Wake lock:** requested after the Start GPS tap, released when the block screen is left, requested again when the page becomes visible and the lock was released; every call is wrapped (a browser without it, or a refusal, is silent).

### C9: findings of the overnight audits (for the manuscript and the docs; nothing here changed a score)
**(a) Squares without soil data are scored without the soil term (3,393 of 8,088 squares, the part outside the LGU soil map).** In `score_sites.py` the soil factor is simply left out of the weighted mean for these squares (it is "not evaluated"; `known_soil` is false). The other three factors (elevation, slope, wetness; weight 0.25 each) are averaged. So the square is NOT punished and it is NOT treated as a "perfect" soil either: its S equals the mean of the other three factors. Checked with an independent script (reports/overnight, not in git): Narra on a square with perfect elevation, slope and wetness gets S = 1.0000 with no soil data, 1.0000 with a matching soil, and 0.8125 with a mismatching soil (factor 0.25). So with unknown soil a square can score higher than the same square with a known mismatching soil. **What the manuscript should say**: "A square outside the LGU soil map (3,393 of 8,088 squares) is scored without the soil term; its score is the average of the other factors, so it is not lowered for unknown soil and may be higher than the score it would have with a known, unsuitable soil. These squares need a field check of the soil before planting." The field kit now says this in Filipino (C6).
**(b) The real effect of the graded slope margin is much smaller than the documented 25%.** Documented: above the species' slope limit S is multiplied by g = 1 - (slope - limit) / (0.25 x limit), so the pair is rejected only beyond limit x 1.25. But the slope factor is already 0 at the limit itself (it falls over the top 10% of the range), so S at the limit is at most (1 + 0 + 1 + 1) / 4 = 0.75, and the pair stays eligible (S >= 0.50) only while S_soft x g >= 0.50: g >= 0.667, that is a slope at most **8.3% of the limit** above the limit (at best), and **2.8% of the limit** in the audit example. Exact numbers found (Narra, limit 30%, soil mismatch): slope 30.0% S 0.5625 eligible; 30.1% S 0.5550 eligible (flag `slope_graded`); 33.75% (limit x 1.125) S 0.2812 not eligible; 37.5% (limit x 1.25) S 0 not eligible; 37.51% rejected by the hard gate. So: the "25%" is the distance of the hard stop, not the width of the window in which a pair can still be recommended. Help and docs that say 25% should say "up to a few percent of the limit".
**(c) "One tree per cell".** Known limit rewritten (C8): a cell holds at most ONE planting block of ONE species; in the default blocks layout a block holds **9 to 900 trees** depending on the planting distance (floor(77.46 m / spacing) per side squared: 20 m = 3 x 3 = 9; 2.5 m = 30 x 30 = 900; the last block of a species holds the remainder); in the points layout one tree per cell, as before.
