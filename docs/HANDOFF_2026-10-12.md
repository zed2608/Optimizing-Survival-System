## 3. The problem found in the review
Both title algorithms work, but the current results do not show that they are needed:
- **Random Forest:** it is trained on the rule scores (`s_rule`) with the same raw inputs, so it copies them (out-of-fold R-squared 0.9998; 168 of 360,450 eligibility decisions change). A Decision Tree is equal (accuracy 0.9979 vs 0.9976). Panel question: "Why not use the rules directly? Why a forest and not a tree?"
- **Hungarian:** `matching_benchmark.csv` (300 trees, whole municipality, automatic palette) shows Hungarian = greedy in all 6 cases, because good squares are plentiful there.

## 4. Evidence found on 12 Oct (run in the chat session; please re-check on the real setup)

### 4a. Hungarian vs greedy in realistic small-area plans
Script: `scripts/scarcity_benchmark_draft.py` (the draft from the chat session; copy it into `scripts/`).
Setup: rule scores from `score_sites.score_pairs` (default graded slope, LGU soil), **wetness NOT evaluated** (`water_dist_m` = NaN, geopandas was unavailable there), P from `purpose_scores.csv`, W = S x P where S >= 0.50, **points layout** (one tree per square), `matching.assign_hungarian` vs `matching.assign_greedy`. 605 random plans: square windows of 5, 8 or 12 cells (about 0.5 to 1.2 km), 2 to 5 user-chosen species, trees = 30%, 60% or 90% of the suitable squares, three purposes, seed 1.

| Result | Hungarian | Greedy |
|---|---|---|
| Trees placed (19,352 asked) | 18,935 | 18,176 |
| Plans where greedy left trees with no spot | | 130 of 605 (21%); 759 trees; median 4, max 35 per plan |
| Plans with a lower total W | | 167 of 605 (28%), up to 36% lower |
| Plans where greedy beat Hungarian | | 0 |
| By tightness (trees / suitable squares) | | 0.3: 7 of 214 plans strand trees; 0.6: 26 of 184; 0.9: 97 of 207 |

Reading: greedy gives a flexible species the only squares a picky species could use, so the picky species' trees get no spot. This is the "I have species" mode with species counts that the system already has. The old benchmark measured the wrong situation.

### 4b. Random Forest on real, independent data: satellite tree cover
Target: `share_tree` from `data/processed/site_landcover.csv` (ESA WorldCover 2021). Squares with `lc_coverage` >= 0.5 and `share_built` < 0.2 (7,392 squares; mean tree share 0.92). Features: elevation, slope, `nearest_feature_dist_m`, LGU soil texture one-hot (plus lat/lon in one run). Spatial GroupKFold(5) on 10 x 10-cell blocks.

| Model | Spatial R-squared (with lat/lon) | Without lat/lon |
|---|---|---|
| Random Forest (200 trees, min leaf 5) | 0.435 +- 0.072 | 0.405 +- 0.091 |
| Linear regression | 0.282 +- 0.078 | |
| Decision Tree (depth 8) | 0.066 +- 0.399 | 0.252 +- 0.084 |

Reading: on real, noisy, nonlinear data the forest clearly beats the tree and the linear model. Caveats: tree cover is not species-specific, it is shaped partly by land-use history, it is the 2021 picture (76.7% accurate worldwide), and the signal is moderate.
