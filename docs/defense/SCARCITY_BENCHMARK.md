# Scarcity benchmark: Hungarian vs greedy vs random feasible (task 1 of the 12 Oct handoff)

**Two parts.** Part 1 (below): whole barangays, blocks and points layouts, `scripts/scarcity_benchmark.py`. Part 2 (last section): small drawn polygons of 5 to 100 squares in the blocks layout, `scripts/scarcity_benchmark_polygons.py`, with its own settings fixed before its first run. Part 2 changes one conclusion of Part 1: in small drawn areas the blocks layout does run short of squares.

Generated by `scripts/scarcity_benchmark.py`; every plan is in `scarcity_benchmark.csv`. The settings below were fixed before the first run and were not changed after seeing results.

## Settings (fixed before the first run)

- **master_seed**: 2026
- **solver_seed**: 42
- **random_repeats**: 20
- **tightness_targets**: (0.3, 0.6, 0.9)
- **replicates**: 4
- **s_sources**: ('rf', 'rules')
- **layouts**: ('blocks', 'points')
- **purposes**: ('urban', 'planting', 'watershed')
- **max_trees**: 2000
- **area**: every one of the 15 barangays, whole barangay, include_unzoned on (the app's default), no field-check exclusions, no planting dates (Habagat off)
- **species_choice**: k species drawn at random, without replacement, from the species that may enter a palette in that area and purpose (palettes.eligible_pool)
- **blocks_counts**: blocks asked B = max(k, round(tightness x U)), split as evenly as possible over the k species; trees of a species = its blocks x its trees per full block
- **points_count**: trees asked N = max(k, round(tightness x U)), at most max_trees; the palette shares them out (caps relaxed as for chosen species)
- **U**: number of squares with S >= 0.50 for at least one of the chosen species

## Setup (one place)

- **S source**: main = `rf` (the Random Forest `s_prob`, the app's default), sensitivity = `rules` (`s_rule`). Used in this run: {'rf': 'rf', 'rules': 'rules'}.
- **Wetness term**: included. The saved scores already contain it (distance to creeks and rivers is known for 100% of the pairs of the pair table).
- **Habagat multiplier**: OFF (no planting dates are given, so no square gets `w_mult`; the script stops if it finds one).
- **Slope rule**: graded (the saved scores' mode). **Zoning**: confirmed + unconfirmed squares (the app's default), 78 excluded squares never scored. No field-check exclusions, no season filter.
- **Release hash**: 34964a09fe44 (v1.0-review). **Squares in the planning context**: {'rf': 8010, 'rules': 8010}.
- **Git**: branch `algorithm-fixes`, HEAD `16af3ca` plus uncommitted work (this script, its test and these outputs). **Date**: 2026-10-12.
- **Code**: production `run_plan.make_plan` for Hungarian and greedy (blocks: `species_counts`; points: `species_ids`); the random feasible baseline is the project's `matching.assign_random` on the same matrix, rebuilt in the script. Production code was not modified.
- **Plans skipped** (not creatable under the rules above): 12 rows; reasons are in the CSV (`status`).

## How to read the tables

Tightness = blocks (or trees, in points layout) asked / squares that suit at least one chosen species. A plan is *scarce* when that number is high. 'Stranded' = asked but not placed. Total W is compared only among plans where both methods place the same number of blocks; plans where they place different numbers are counted separately, because Hungarian first places as many as possible and only then maximizes W.

## What the numbers show (written by hand after the run; the tables above are generated)

**Run history (honest note).** The script was run twice with identical settings. The first run reported 694 cross-check failures: its tolerance for the 4-decimal rounding that `make_plan` applies to every planted row was too strict for plans with many rows. The placed blocks and trees were identical in every case (checked on sampled cases); only the tolerance and the way W is reported were fixed (the comparison now uses the exact rebuilt W, the production total is kept in `total_W_production`). No setting or seed changed, and I had not looked at any result table before the fix. The second run has 0 failures.

**1. Blocks layout (the main table): no scarcity occurs, so there is nothing to strand.** In 534 plans no method leaves a single tree without a spot. The achieved tightness has a median of 0.10 (90th percentile 0.49; 53 plans reach 0.5 and only 5 reach 0.9). The reason is structural: a block holds 9 to 900 trees, a plan is limited to 2,000 trees, so a plan needs few squares and the barangays have many (12 skipped cases would need more than 2,000 trees even with one block per species). Hungarian is not worse and greedy is never better, but the only visible difference is the total W: greedy gives a lower total W in 35 of 534 plans (rf; largest gap 2.70%; 17 plans and 5.50% with the rules), and never a higher one. The average W per block is almost the same (0.4799 against 0.4797).

**2. Points layout (the second table, one tree per square): scarcity appears, and Hungarian matters there.** At the tightest level (0.9) greedy places fewer blocks than Hungarian in 31 of 180 plans; at 0.6 in 4 of 180; at 0.3 in 1 of 180; in total 36 of 540 plans (7%). Hungarian never places fewer than greedy (0 of 540). Trees stranded by the matching itself: Hungarian 16, greedy 1,085, random feasible 309 (of 127,630 trees asked; the other 3,482 stranded trees are caused by the palette step and are the same for every method). The worst plan strands 87 more blocks with greedy than with Hungarian (310 trees). Among the 504 plans where both place the same number of blocks, greedy has a lower total W in 191 (largest gap 4.93%) and a higher one in none. The same picture with the rules as S source: greedy places fewer blocks in 37 of 540 plans, Hungarian in none, and the trees stranded by the matching are Hungarian 16, greedy 1,093, random feasible 308. The S source makes no meaningful difference to this comparison (nor to the blocks table).

**3. Surprise: greedy is worse than random feasible at not stranding trees** (1,085 against 309 trees stranded by the matching; at tightness 0.9: 2,491 against 1,755 stranded in all). Greedy always gives the best square to the species with the highest W, which uses up the squares that a picky species needs.

**4. The effect is smaller than the chat draft's** (21% of plans with stranded trees, 28% with lower total W, in 605 random square windows with the points layout). Here it is 7% and 38% in whole barangays. The draft chose small windows (0.5 to 1.2 km) and one-tree-per-square plans at 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 of the suitable squares; this run uses the real barangays, the real palette (caps) and the saved S with wetness, so the two are not directly comparable. I did not test which of these differences explains the gap.

**5. Where it happens:** 29 of the 36 plans are in barangays of 500 squares or fewer (Maly 10, Malanday 6, Silangan 6, Guitnang Bayan 5), 7 in the 72 plans of larger areas; spread over all three purposes (13, 12, 11) and all species counts k = 2 to 5 (6, 11, 10, 9).

**6. Runtime is not a reason to choose greedy:** the solver alone takes a median of 0.2 to 0.3 ms for Hungarian and 0.3 to 1.2 ms for greedy; a whole `make_plan` call takes about 35 to 42 ms for either.

**What Part 1 does and does not support.** It supports: *Hungarian is needed when squares are scarce for the chosen species*, which in whole barangays happens in the points layout (API and command line only), mostly in smaller areas and at high tightness. In the blocks layout of whole barangays it is not needed to place the trees (no stranded trees in 534 plans; a small total-W gain in 7% of plans). Part 2 (last section) shows that the blocks layout does run short of squares in small drawn areas, so the claim also holds for plans a dashboard user can make. Both parts use designed tightness levels (0.3, 0.6, 0.9) and random species sets, and there are no usage data: how often real planners meet such tight cases is unknown.

## Which part of the app produces the points layout, and can a normal user reach it?

**Short answer: no. A normal dashboard user can only get the blocks layout.** Checked in the code on 12 Oct 2026:

- **The dashboard never asks for the points layout.** The plan request it builds (`frontend/src/new/AppNew.jsx`, `planBody`) contains only the purpose, the area, and either `n_saplings` (goal "I have an area") or `species_counts` (goal "I have species"). It has no `layout_mode` field, and no screen, button, setting or help text offers a choice of layout. The words "points layout" or "one tree per square" appear nowhere in the dashboard's screens.
- **The service then uses its default, which is blocks:** `API_CFG["layout_mode"]` (`api_v2.py`) is taken from `run_plan.CFG["layout_mode"] = "blocks"`. So the same two goals always give block plans, and the progress, "Planted" status and top-up features exist only for block plans.
- **Ways to get points plans (none of them on a screen):**
  1. the API: send `"layout_mode": "points"` in `POST /plan-event` (it is documented in the interactive API page at `http://localhost:8001/docs`; with `species_counts` too, a case that `tests/test_species_counts.py` covers);
  2. the command line: `python pipeline/run_plan.py --layout-mode points`;
  3. a code or configuration change of the default (`API_CFG["layout_mode"]` or `run_plan.CFG["layout_mode"]`);
  4. plans saved before round 10a (5 Oct 2026) load as points plans; three 300-tree plans of that date are in `data/processed/plans/`.
- The old dashboard (`#/legacy`) does not use these algorithms at all (its scores are computed in JavaScript).

**What this means for the wording of the thesis.**
- The result that matters for users in **Part 1** is the **blocks table**: in whole-barangay plans no method strands a tree (0 of 534 plans) and Hungarian gives only a small total-W gain in 35 of 534 plans (largest 2.70%). Do not write that Hungarian prevents stranded trees in whole-barangay plans on the basis of Part 1; for small drawn areas see Part 2, where it does.
- The **points table** is a **stress test of the matching algorithm** on the earlier one-tree-per-square formulation, which the code still contains and which the API and the command line can still run. It shows what the Hungarian algorithm does when squares are scarce (greedy strands 1,085 trees against 16, in 36 of 540 plans, 31 of them at the highest tightness). It supports the claim "Hungarian is the right solver when a plan competes for scarce squares", not the claim "users meet this situation in the dashboard".
- Honest framing for Part 1: *"In whole-barangay plans in the block layout used by the dashboard, squares are rarely scarce and greedy and Hungarian place the same trees; with one tree per square (API and command line) greedy strands up to 87 blocks more in a plan."* Whether a block-based plan can become scarce was not found in these 534 whole-barangay plans; **Part 2 tests small drawn areas, which the dashboard does offer, and finds that it does (greedy places fewer blocks in 13.5% of the plans).**

### Two things to keep in mind when reading these tables

- **One grid square is about 1 ha (100 m x 100 m).** A real school yard, a roadside strip or a garden smaller than one square cannot be represented: the smallest area here (5 squares) is about 5 ha, and a plan always uses whole squares.
- **Separate plans do not exclude each other's squares; only top-ups do.** Two plans drawn over the same polygon get the same squares (and a polygon already planted is not removed from a new plan). So scarcity in these tables comes from the size of the polygon and the species chosen, not from earlier plans.

### What the polygon numbers show (written by hand after the run; the tables above are generated)

**Result in one line: in small drawn areas, greedy places fewer blocks than Hungarian in 121 of 893 plans (13.5%), Hungarian never places fewer than greedy (0 of 893), and greedy never beats it on total W.** The two S sources give almost the same picture (rules: the same 121 of 893).

1. **How much.** Over all 893 plans (rf) the plans ask for 1,054,209 trees. Hungarian places 1,028,238 and greedy 991,867, so greedy strands 36,371 more trees (3.5% of the trees asked). In one plan greedy places 9 fewer blocks than Hungarian (up to 1,501 more trees without a spot).
2. **By area size** (plans where greedy places fewer blocks, of about 178 per size): 5 squares 22, 10 squares 28, 25 squares 37, 50 squares 30, 100 squares 4. The effect is largest at 25 squares and nearly gone at 100 squares (Hungarian places everything there, greedy misses 821 trees in 4 plans). For comparison, in whole barangays (part 1, blocks layout) it was 0 of 534.
3. **By tightness asked for:** 22 of 296 plans at 0.3, 28 of 300 at 0.6, and 71 of 297 at 0.9 (24%). It grows with tightness, as expected. By number of species chosen: 19 (k = 2), 30 (k = 3), 33 (k = 4), 39 (k = 5); by purpose: urban 34, planting 39, watershed 48.
4. **Two kinds of plan.** In 75 of the 121 plans Hungarian places every block that was asked for and greedy leaves trees without a spot (24,384 trees saved). In the other 46, nobody can place everything (87 of the 893 plans ask for more blocks than a method can place, 28 of them at 5 squares); there greedy places even fewer than Hungarian. Part of this stranding is therefore a request the area cannot meet, which no solver can fix; the app's plan form already warns when the blocks asked exceed the suitable squares.
5. **Total W** (only plans where both place the same number of blocks, 772 of them): greedy is lower in 58 (largest gap 12.53%) and higher in none; with the rules 47 and 12.55%.
6. **Same surprise as in part 1:** greedy is no better than random feasible. It strands more trees than random feasible at every size (for example 21,150 against 14,549 at 25 squares), and in 114 plans it places fewer blocks than the random baseline's average (random is lower in 85). Hungarian places more blocks than random feasible's average in 198 plans and never fewer.
7. **Runtime is not an issue:** the solver alone takes about 0.1 to 0.2 ms for every method, `make_plan` about 31 to 36 ms, `plan_scope` with the polygon about 1 ms.

**Can a user reach this?** Yes. Unlike the points layout, this case uses only what the dashboard offers: goal "I have an area", the "Draw an area" tool, and "Choose my own species and counts" (the plan request then carries `polygon` and `species_counts`, with the default blocks layout).

**What this means for the claim "Hungarian is needed".** For plans in small drawn areas with several chosen species and counts, a simple greedy assignment fails to place trees that an optimal assignment places in about one plan in eight, and in about one in four when the request is tight (0.9). This supports the claim for that case, in the blocks layout the app really uses. It does not change part 1: for whole barangays the blocks plans never ran short of squares. So the honest statement is: *Hungarian is needed when the area is small relative to the blocks the chosen species need, which a user can create by drawing a small area; for barangay-sized areas it makes no difference to the number of trees placed.*

**Caveats.** (a) The tightness levels (0.3, 0.6, 0.9 of the suitable squares), the 2 to 5 random species and the compact polygon shapes were designed for this benchmark; real drawn areas may be long strips or cross zone borders, and real planners may pick species and counts differently. (b) How often users draw areas of 5 to 50 squares (5 to 50 ha) is unknown; there are no usage data. (c) A school yard or a strip smaller than one square (about 1 ha) cannot be represented at all; the smallest area tested is 5 squares. (d) 14 plan cases (7 per S source) were skipped because even one block per species would need more than 2,000 trees. (e) No tuning: sizes, seeds, tightness levels and the number of polygons were fixed before the first run.
