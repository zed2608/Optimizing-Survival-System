## Settings (fixed before the first run)

- **seed**: 42
- **outer_folds**: 5
- **inner_folds**: 3
- **block_cells**: 10
- **block_cells_sensitivity**: 20
- **target**: share_tree (ESA WorldCover 2021, data/processed/site_landcover.csv)
- **squares**: scored squares (confirmed + outside the zoning map) with lc_coverage >= 0.5 and share_built < 0.2; the 2 squares without a slope value are left out of training (never filled in); all other squares get NO potential and stay neutral
- **features_main**: elev_m, slope_pct, LGU soil texture (one-hot: Clay, Clay Loam, Loam, Sandy Loam, Silt Loam, plus an indicator 'no soil texture')
- **variants**: main = the features above | nf = main + nearest_feature_dist_m | latlon = main + longitude + latitude | nosoil = elevation and slope only | blk20 = main, blocks of 20 x 20 cells (about 2 km)
- **models**: RandomForest (200 trees; tuned: max_depth {12, None} x min_samples_leaf {1, 5}), DecisionTree (tuned: max_depth {4, 8, 16, None}), KNN (standardised; tuned: k {5, 15, 31, 61}), linear regression (standardised, no tuning), mean-only baseline; same 3-fold spatial inner tuning for the tuned ones
- **folds**: GroupKFold(5, shuffle, seed 42) over blocks of 10 x 10 grid cells, built by compare_models.make_folds (the same block definition and seed as the other model comparisons; the fold assignment differs because the table has one row per square, not per pair)
- **metrics**: R-squared, MAE, RMSE per fold; mean and standard deviation (n - 1) over the 5 folds
- **low_tree**: squares with share_tree < 0.5: ranking check (AUROC; precision and recall among the 10% and 20% of squares with the lowest predicted potential), descriptive only
- **adjust_w_factors**: (0.05, 0.1, 0.2)
- **adjust_w_form**: W' = W x (1 - a x (1 - potential)); potential clipped to 0..1; no potential -> factor 1; S and the S >= 0.50 test are untouched
- **tiebreak_form**: matching cost + 1e-6 x ((1 - potential) + 0.001 x distance to the area centre); no potential -> the area's median potential (neutral); the same 1e-6 weight as the existing distance tie-break (not tuned)
- **gate_plans**: the plans of scarcity_benchmark.py (blocks layout, species_counts, rf source, 15 barangays) and scarcity_benchmark_polygons.py (100 drawn polygons), same seeds; Hungarian matching

Data: 7,322 of 8,010 scored squares were used (the others stay without a potential: 686 built-up or low-coverage, 2 without slope). Target: mean tree share 0.926, 81% of squares at 0.95 or more, 430 squares below 0.5 (mostly grassland and cropland).

## Reading (written by hand after the run; the tables above are generated and the pre-registration was not changed)

**1. The pre-registered verdict is DROP, and it does not hinge on a rounding.** R2 is on the line: the forest's mean R-squared is 0.29999 (per fold 0.067, 0.354, 0.614, 0.337, 0.128), which is below 0.30 by about 0.00001, so by the rule it fails. But R1 fails by a clear margin, independent of R2: the forest is only 0.040 above the Decision Tree (needed 0.10) and 0.084 above KNN (needed 0.10). It does beat linear regression by 0.158. R3 passes (-0.221).

**2. The forest is not distinguishable from the simpler models here.** The fold-to-fold standard deviation is about 0.22, far larger than the differences between forest, tree and KNN (0.04 to 0.08). The forest is ahead in every fold (5 of 5 against each), but by little; the R-squared of one fold ranges from 0.07 to 0.61, so how well the environment predicts tree cover depends strongly on where you look. Without soil, KNN (0.285) is slightly above the forest (0.273). The one clear gain is over linear regression (0.30 against 0.14): the relationship is not linear.

**3. Sensitivities.** Adding `nearest_feature_dist_m` (0.292) or longitude and latitude (0.295) does not help; leaving out soil costs only 0.03, so the 46% of squares without soil texture (which are more forested, 0.97 against 0.89) are not what drives the result. With 2 km blocks the forest falls to 0.211: the model does not carry well to land farther away.

**4. It does recognise open land, which is the opposite of the intention.** On the 430 squares with tree cover below 0.5 (grassland and cropland) the forest separates them from the rest with an AUROC of 0.865: the lowest 10% of predicted potential holds 38.8% low-cover squares (base rate 5.9%) and catches 66% of all of them. The mean predicted potential of those squares is 0.66 against 0.95 for the others. So even though the potential is a *prediction* from elevation, slope and soil, it tracks where open land already is, and using it in plans would in practice favour already-wooded ground over open land. That conflicts with "never penalize open land" and is a reason against the layer, not for it. (Not a drop rule.)

**5. It does not copy the rules; if anything it points the other way.** The correlation with the rule score is -0.22 (-0.32 with the number of eligible species): squares the rules like most (lowland, gentle) have lower predicted potential, probably because the forest and the steeper upland go together (a guess, not tested).

**6. Decision gate: what it would change in the plans (rf source, Hungarian; nothing is built).**
- **Placed blocks and trees: no plan changes, in any variant** (0 of 534 barangay plans and 0 of 893 polygon plans). This is by construction: none of the variants touches S or the 0.50 test, and the number of blocks the matching can place depends only on which squares are eligible.
- **Tie-break** (potential first, distance second): total W does not change (0.0000%), but it moves **54.3%** of the blocks of barangay plans and **40.0%** of the blocks of polygon plans to other squares, raising the mean potential of the chosen squares from 0.744 to 0.820 (barangays) and from 0.909 to 0.934 (polygons). It also **spreads the blocks out**: the mean distance of the blocks from the area centre becomes 1.67 times (barangays) and 1.50 times (polygons) larger, because the potential replaces the distance as the first tie-break and so removes the compactness the current tie-break gives. A distance-first version was not run. There is much room for a tie-break: 89.4% (barangays) and 80.7% (polygons) of the chosen blocks have another feasible square of the same species within the 1e-6 tie width, because `s_prob` is stored with 4 decimals and many squares share the same value; today those choices are decided by the distance to the centre and a seeded order.
- **adjust_w** (three factors, none chosen): 41% to 49% of the blocks move to other squares; total W falls by 0.009%, 0.024% and 0.034% in the barangay plans (factors 0.05, 0.10, 0.20) and by 0.002%, 0.003% and 0.005% in the polygon plans; the worst single plan loses 1.5% in the barangay plans and 1.1% in the polygon plans at factor 0.20 (0.4% to 0.94% at the two smaller factors). The chosen squares rise from 0.744 to 0.77 and from 0.909 to 0.942 in mean potential, again with blocks spread about 1.5 times as far from the centre.
- So the layer would **rearrange where the blocks go (40% to 54% of them) at almost no cost in W, but would not change how many trees or blocks are placed**, and the effect is mostly among near-identical squares.
- The baseline of this script reproduces the saved benchmark in 534 of 534 and 893 of 893 plans.

**7. What this means.** Under the rules written down before the run, the idea is dropped: the forest does not clearly beat a Decision Tree or KNN on real data, and R-squared 0.30 is moderate with a very large spread between regions. A potential-first tie-break would change many placements but at the price of compact blocks, and the layer partly acts as a map of existing open land. What can still be said honestly in the thesis: *on a real, noisy target (satellite tree cover) a nonlinear model predicts clearly better than a linear one (R-squared 0.30 against 0.14, same spatial folds), but the forest is not clearly better than a single tree or KNN.* Whether to build the switch anyway is Ayzhiedy's and the adviser's decision, not the benchmark's.

**Limits.** Tree cover is not species-specific, reflects land-use history and the 2021 picture (76.7% accurate worldwide), and there are still no survival records; only environment features that already enter the rules were available, so no independent information was added; 686 built-up squares and 2 squares without slope stay without a potential; the gate analysis uses the species-counts benchmark plans (fixed palette), so effects on an automatic species mix were not measured.
